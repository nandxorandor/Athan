Before you start

  • A Google account you'll keep. The developer account is tied to it for good.
  • A credit or debit card in your legal name. Prepaid cards are refused.
  • A government ID (passport or national ID). Google checks it against the name you register.
  • An Android phone. New accounts are asked to sign in to the Play Console app on a real device.
  • Your app, building in Android Studio, targeting a recent Android version. Google's minimum moves up every year (see Target API level requirements in Play Console Help). The Console refuses an upload below it.
  • A privacy policy at a public web address. A page on GitHub Pages or your own site is enough. It's required even if your app collects nothing.
  • At least 12 people who can test the app for two weeks (more in stage 6).

Personal or organization account?

Personal is the usual choice for one developer. Since November 2023, new personal accounts must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days, separately for each app, before it can go public. Organization accounts skip that rule, but need a D-U-N-S number for a registered business, which takes days or weeks to get.

1

Open a developer account

  1. Go to play.google.com/console/signup and sign in with the Google account you chose.
  2. Choose Yourself for a personal account, or An organization.
  3. Developer name: shown publicly under your app in the store. It can be your name or a brand.
  4. Contact details: a contact email and phone number. Google sends a code to each; enter both to verify.
  5. Legal name and address: exactly as on your ID.
  6. Answer the short questions about your experience and how many apps you plan to publish. Any honest answer is fine.
  7. Accept the Developer Distribution Agreement and pay the US$25 registration fee. It's charged once for the account, no matter how many apps you publish later.
  8. Identity verification: upload a photo of your ID when asked. Approval usually takes from a few hours to a few days; you get an email.
  9. Device verification: install the Google Play Console app on your Android phone, sign in with the same account and follow the prompt.
Check what you paidThe fee appears in your Google payments history at payments.google.com → Activity as "Developer Registration Fee". If you see it twice, you opened two accounts.
2

Build a signed .aab

Google Play only accepts an Android App Bundle (.aab), not an .apk.

  1. In app/build.gradle(.kts), set a unique applicationId (for example com.yourname.appname). It can never be changed once uploaded. Set versionCode = 1 and versionName = "1.0".
  2. In Android Studio: Build›Generate Signed App Bundle or APK…
  3. Choose Android App Bundle → Next.
  4. Under Key store path, click Create new…. Pick where to save the file (.jks), set a password, an alias (e.g. upload), a key password, validity of 25+ years, and your name in the certificate fields. Click OK.
  5. Click Next, choose the release build variant, click Create. The file appears in app/release/app-release.aab.
Back up the key file and its passwords nowEvery future update must be signed with this same key. Copy the .jks file and its passwords somewhere off this computer (not on GitHub). If you lose it, Google can reset your upload key, but only through support, and it takes days.
Play App SigningOn your first upload, Google keeps the real app-signing key and your key becomes the "upload key". This is automatic. It means a copy installed from Play and an .apk you sign yourself have different signatures: one can't be installed over the other.
3

Create the app in the Console

  1. Open play.google.com/console. On Home, click Create app (top right).
  2. App name (up to 30 characters), Default language, App or game, Free or paid. A free app can never be changed to paid later.
  3. Tick the declarations (Developer Program Policies, US export laws) and click Create app.
  4. You land on the app's Dashboard. It holds a checklist of setup tasks; each one links to the right page. Stages 4 and 5 below are that checklist.
4

App content forms

All under Monitor and improve›Policy and programs›App content. Each one: open it, answer, Save.

  1. Privacy policy: paste your policy's URL → Save.
  2. App access: if everything works without logging in, choose All functionality is available without special access. Otherwise give Google test login details.
  3. Ads: does the app show ads? Yes or no.
  4. Content rating: Start questionnaire → enter an email → pick the category (e.g. Reference, News, or Educational or Utility) → answer the questions about violence, language etc. → Save → Next → Submit. The ratings (ESRB, PEGI…) are calculated from your answers.
  5. Target audience and content: pick the age groups. Choosing any group under 13 brings in the extra Families policy; pick 13+ or 18+ unless the app really is for children.
  6. Data safety: the longest form. Declare what data the app collects or shares (location, contacts, crash logs…), why, whether it's encrypted in transit, and whether users can request deletion. If your app sends anything off the phone (even coordinates to a weather service) it counts. Review the preview → Submit.
  7. Advertising ID: say whether the app uses it. If you have no ads and no analytics SDK, usually no.
  8. Government apps, Financial features, Health, News: answer no unless they apply.
  9. Permission declarations: some permissions only show up as a task after you upload a bundle, for example exact alarms, foreground services, full-screen notifications, background location or "all files" access. Each asks why the app needs it, sometimes with a short video. Answer them before releasing.
5

Store listing

  1. Grow users›Store presence›Store settings: choose the category (e.g. Lifestyle, Tools), add tags, and the contact email users will see (website and phone are optional). Save.
  2. Grow users›Store presence›Store listings → click Default store listing.
  3. Fill the text, then upload the graphics (sizes below), then Save at the bottom.
  4. To change an image later: hover over it → trash icon → upload the new one → Save.
  5. Optional: Add translations for a listing in another language (e.g. Arabic).

6

Closed test: 12 testers, 14 days

Required for new personal accounts, for each app. Internal testing does not count toward it.

Set up the track

  1. Test and release›Testing›Closed testing. Open the default track (usually Alpha) or click Create track.
  2. Countries / regions tab → Add countries / regions → tick where your testers are (or all) → Save.
  3. Testers tab → Create email list → name it → type each tester's Gmail address and press Enter after each one → Save changes.
  4. Back on the Testers tab, tick the checkbox next to your list, add a feedback email or URL, and click Save.
"Save changes" stays greyThe address in the box isn't added until you press Enter. It has to move down into the "Email addresses added" box first.

Create the release

  1. Releases tab → Create new release.
  2. Under App bundles click Upload and choose your .aab. The release name fills in by itself (e.g. 1 (1.0)).
  3. Release notes: type between the language tags, for example: 
    <en-US>
First release.
</en-US>
    The whole box is limited to 500 characters, tags included.
  4. Next → read any errors (must fix) and warnings (can ignore) → Save.
  5. Go to Publishing overview and click Send changes for review if it's offered. Google runs quick checks and then reviews; the first review can take a few days.

Get the testers in

  1. When the release is live on the track, go to the Testers tab and copy the Join on Android link (or Join on the web).
  2. Send it to each tester. They open it signed in with the same Gmail you listed, tap Become a tester, then install the app from the Play Store page it links to.
  3. They must stay opted in for 14 days in a row. If the number drops below 12, the count can restart. The Dashboard shows your progress.
  4. Ask testers to actually open the app a few times and send feedback. Google later asks you what you learned from the test.
Where to find 12 testersFriends and family with Gmail accounts, a developer community (members testing each other's apps), or a paid tester service. Paid services cost roughly US$15–25 per app and run the 14 days for you.
7

Apply for production access

  1. Once you have 12 testers and 14 days, the Dashboard shows Apply for production. Click it.
  2. Answer the questions about the closed test (how you found testers, how hard it was, what feedback you got and what you changed), about the app (who it's for, what it does) and whether it's ready. Be specific; short vague answers are a common reason for rejection.
  3. Submit. Google says the review usually takes up to 7 days. If it's refused, the email says why; fix that, keep testing, apply again.
8

Release to production

  1. Test and release›Production → Countries / regions tab → Add countries / regions → select all, or the ones you want → Save.
  2. Releases tab → Create new release.
  3. Click Add from library and pick the bundle your testers used, then Add to release. No need to upload it again.
  4. Paste the release notes → Next → Save.
  5. Publishing overview → Send changes for review. Google runs quick checks (about 15 minutes) and then the full review: from a few hours to several days for a first production release.
  6. If Managed publishing is on (toggle at the top of Publishing overview), approval doesn't publish anything: you have to click Publish yourself. If it's off, the app goes live as soon as it's approved.
  7. Check Publishing overview›Submission activity. When your submission says Published, open it to confirm it contains your Production release, not just settings. Your store page is play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=YOUR.APPLICATION.ID. Search results can take a few more hours.
9

Publishing updates

  1. Raise versionCode by at least 1 (and versionName as you like). Play refuses a bundle with a version code it has seen before.
  2. Build a new signed .aab with the same key.
  3. Optional but wise: put it on Testing›Internal testing first. That track has no long review and reaches up to 100 testers, so you can install exactly what Play will deliver.
  4. Production›Create new release → upload, or Add from library if you already uploaded it to internal testing → notes → Next → Save → Send changes for review.
  5. Updates are usually reviewed within hours. You can also release to a percentage of users first (a staged rollout) and raise it later.
!

Traps we fell into

"Published", but nothing in the storeA submission marked Published can contain only settings, such as a countries list. Open it in Submission activity and check it lists a release under Production. Until a production release exists, the store link shows "URL not found" and the Dashboard says Production: Inactive.
Released to the wrong trackOpen testing, Closed testing and Production all have a "Create new release" button that looks the same. Check the page title before you upload.
In-app language switch stops workingFrom an .aab, Play installs only the languages set on the phone. If your app lets users pick a language themselves, the other language's text is missing. Fix it in app/build.gradle.kts: 
android {
    bundle {
        language { enableSplit = false }
    }
}
Play copy vs. your own APKBecause Google re-signs the Play copy, users who installed your .apk from GitHub or a website can't update it from Play. They have to uninstall it first, which clears its settings. Tell them in your release notes.
Release notes too longThe 500-character limit covers the whole box, so English and Arabic notes together must fit in 500.
